The wheel should let you turn without crouching or crushing the rim. Grip force is part of the driver setup: too hard and you stop feeling; too soft and you lose precision.

## The idea

Height, angle, and reach decide whether you fight or read the car. A practical target: neutral wrists, supported shoulders, ability to turn a wide range without elbows hitting the seat.

Grip is measured on a subjective scale (e.g. 3/10 vs 7/10). You train the lowest value that still gives apex precision.

## Why it works

The front tire speaks through force and vibration. Crush the rim and that message dies—you answer late. Light hands bring countersteer earlier and cut the total angle you need.

Under braking, a death grip sneaks residual steering degrees and breaks the 50/50 balance you need for a longitudinal peak. So “light hands under braking” is not a separate tip: it starts with how you hold the wheel all the time.

## On track

A medium-speed corner where the rear often steps at mid-load. At grip 9/10 countersteer arrives after peak yaw. At 4/10 you catch it earlier with fewer degrees.

If you “fix” that by raising FFB, it often gets worse: more fight, more fatigue. First loosen the hands; then, if needed, touch FFB.

## Typical mistakes

- Wheel too low or far “because it looks good”
- Changing FFB instead of releasing grip
- Iron grip only at brake peak (the worst moment)
- Crossing arms into the seat and blaming the car

<Exercise>
Same corner, **4 laps** at ~3/10 grip and **4 laps** at ~7/10 (subjective scale).

Note which one lets you feel oversteer onset sooner. Keep the lowest value that still gives apex precision. Use it as default next session.
