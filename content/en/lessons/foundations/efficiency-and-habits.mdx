Going fast is not spending more energy—it is spending less fighting the car. Efficiency is the filter for posture, wheel grip, and every habit that repeats under braking.

## The idea

Muscle memory does not judge. It repeats what you do most often—good or bad. An efficient habit gives you **more information per meter** (tire feedback, FFB, seat) with less muscular and mental tension.

Efficient does not mean “soft”. It means effort goes to the useful input: metering brake, reading the front axle, correcting with little angle. Everything else (high shoulders, death grip, staring at the hood) is cost with no return.

## Why it works

Under braking and load, the body goes stiff by default. Tight hands filter tire vibration. Raised shoulders delay countersteer. Short vision increases anxiety and late corrections.

If you train a short checklist—shoulders down, light grip, eyes far—you build a habit that **amplifies** the car’s message. Then you can ask for more pace without tension stealing the threshold.

## On track

You notice that at every brake peak you squeeze the wheel. The car stays “straight”, but you arrive at turn-in without feeling the threshold—you only felt your forearms. The habit fights the car instead of reading it.

Change the association: **brake peak = light hands**. Not a motivational line; a trigger you practice on purpose for a whole block, even if time rises a little.

## Typical mistakes

- Correcting with more wheel force instead of less
- Talking about “feel” without a measurable checklist
- Raising FFB to “feel more” when the problem is hand grip
- Long sessions without rest: fatigue reinstalls the death grip

<Exercise>
On a long straight, do **10 stops** or slows to ~40 km/h.

Before each one say aloud: *shoulders down, grip 4/10, eyes on the horizon*.

If you miss the checklist, that rep does not count. Log how many of 10 you hit. Goal: 8/10.
</Exercise>
