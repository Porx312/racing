Sustainable speed needs a body and mind that can hold the limit. A six-hour Sunday with no method loses to short blocks with a clear **+1%** per session.

## The idea

The +1% is a small, verifiable change: eyes a bit farther, a cleaner brake peak, more linear throttle in one corner. It is not “get better in general”. It is a checkbox you can tick at the end of the block.

Fitness here is not only the gym (though it helps): it is managing fatigue, hydration, rests, and tension so the nervous system still delivers precision.

## Why it works

Fatigue delays the brake foot and hardens the hands. That creates big corrections, which burn more energy. The loop feeds itself.

Fifteen-minute blocks with rest break the loop. One +1% per block avoids saturating attention. After a week you have several layers installed without the feeling of “I improved nothing in three straight hours”.

## On track

Same chicane, same mark. After 40 minutes without a break you brake 5 m later and the wheel “pulls”. It is not the setup—it is the nervous system asking for less precision. If in that state you “try a new technique”, you mix fatigue with learning and never know what failed.

Better: three blocks with a different +1% and a real rest between them. If the third block is worse, stop: practicing tired records garbage.

## Typical mistakes

- Marathon sessions with no focus and no rest
- Changing setup and technique on the same day
- Stacking five “small” changes that are actually a full redesign
- Ignoring neck/wrist pain: posture is part of the +1%

<Exercise>
Three **15-minute** blocks with **3-minute** rests.

Each block a different +1%, for example:

1. Eyes farther on the braking straight
2. Cleaner brake peak (no double pump)
3. More linear throttle on one specific exit

Log yes/no per block. Do not stack more than two technique changes on the same calendar day.
</Exercise>
