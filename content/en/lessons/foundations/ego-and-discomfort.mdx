Ego wants the best time **now**. Learning needs a slightly lower pace to install a new technique. If you do not manage that tension, you will quit every drill the moment you lose two tenths.

## The idea

There are two different discomforts:

- **Useful:** not knowing yet; the car feels weird because the input is new.
- **Toxic:** fighting the car so you do not “lose” a lap to a ghost or a lobby.

Useful discomfort is bought by dropping to ~95% of your usual pace and measuring the technical goal, not the ranking. Toxic discomfort feeds on comparing yourself while you are still in lab mode.

## Why it works

At 100% of your current limit there is no mental or physical grip left to vary trail, angle, or Press Less. You need grip and attention margin. That margin does not appear “when you feel ready”—you create it on purpose.

Fear and ego block learning: you return to the old style because time went up. The old style is already automated; the new one is not. If you quit, it never wins.

## On track

Half a session you are fastest in the lobby. You introduce a more conscious brake release and lose 0.4 s. Ego wants the old hit back. If you comply, the clean peak never records.

The correct session: turn off the comparison, keep one goal (“release without a step”), and accept worse times for a measurable block. At the end you evaluate whether the goal was hit—not whether you won the server.

## Typical mistakes

- Quitting a drill because time rises on the first reps
- Comparing yourself to a faster ghost mid-learning
- Adding a new setup and a new technique the same day “to get the time back”
- Telling yourself you are “already fast” and skipping fundamentals

<Exercise>
One **20-minute** session at ~95% of your usual pace.

- One technical goal only (e.g. lighter hands under braking).
- No ranking or ghost.

At the end, log only this: did you hit the goal on **8 of 10** laps? Yes or no.
</Exercise>
