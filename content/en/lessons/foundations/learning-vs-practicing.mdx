If you rack up laps with no goal, you are not learning—you are recording your current level. This lesson separates **learning** (deliberately changing one input) from **practicing** (repeating until that change runs on its own). It is the base of the whole syllabus.

## The idea

Learning means forcing one concrete variable: turn-in point, brake peak, throttle smoothness. Practicing means repeating that pattern until it no longer occupies conscious attention. You need both, in different proportions.

A useful work rule: spend a small fraction of the session **introducing** something new, and the rest **internalizing** it. Learn without repetition and you never automate. Practice without focus and you lock in mistakes with more confidence.

## Why it works

The car answers what your hands and feet already know how to do. Under load, the conscious mind can watch only one or two things at once. If you try to “fix” braking, line, and throttle on the same lap, you isolate nothing: grip and the clock become noise.

When you pick a single variable, the tire and sector time tell you whether the change works. When that variable comes out without thinking, you free attention for the next layer—traffic, temperature, a harder corner.

## On track

Picture a 90° hairpin with a clear curb mark. Today you are not hunting lap time. You are hunting turn-in only: same entry speed, same brake percentage, and you move the steer half a meter earlier or later. Everywhere else you drive on “good enough” autopilot—no analysis.

That is a learning session. When the new turn-in stays stable for several laps, you shift to practice: reps with that single focus until it stops feeling forced.

## Typical mistakes

- “Warm-up” laps without saying the block goal out loud
- Changing three things at once and not knowing which one improved the sector
- Quitting a drill because lap time rose 0.2 s on the first clean lap
- Watching the ranking or ghost while you are still installing the technique

<Exercise>
Pick **one** corner and **one** metric (sector time or minimum speed).

1. 5 laps measuring only the turn-in point (curb mark).
2. 5 laps measuring only peak brake pressure (0–100%).
3. 5 laps combining both.

Note which block lowered the sector. Do not look at full lap time until all three blocks are done.
</Exercise>
