Posture is not cosmetics: it is the channel for car information and the path for corrections. Sit poorly and everything else trains crooked.

## The idea

You want a stable compromise: back supported under braking, shoulders that do not leave the seat when you turn, arms almost straight with wrists able to rest on the top of the rim, full brake without the torso launching forward.

Do not blindly copy a streamer’s angles. Use criteria: can I brake hard without arching my back? Can I turn without pulling the wheel toward me?

## Why it works

Very bent arms exaggerate steering angle. Over-stretched arms make you pull the rim. Without firm support, your body becomes a damper and filters front-axle messages.

In the sim, FFB and load-cell pedals punish bad geometry: early fatigue, kinked wrists, “setup oversteer” that is really your torso moving.

## On track

Seat too far: in a tight corner you lean in and pull. The car rotates too much exactly when you needed light hands. It feels like setup oversteer; it is body geometry.

Adjust seat first, pedals second, wheel last. Then drive laps only noticing whether the brake peak “weighs” less on the wheel because you are no longer bracing with your hands.

## Typical mistakes

- Copying a video posture without testing under hard braking
- Raising the seat “to see better” and losing lumbar support
- Wheel too low “F1 style” on a rig that cannot support it
- Changing five cockpit settings in one session

<Exercise>
Adjust until you can rest your wrists on the rim with shoulders on the seat and arms almost straight. Floor the brake without your back leaving the seat.

Drive **5 laps** only noticing whether the wheel weighs less at brake peak. Log yes/no. If no, move the seat or wheel closer—do not raise FFB.
</Exercise>
