Before you brake later, you need the car’s **ceiling**. Mechanical grip is the base: weight on the asphalt, without relying on wings.

## The idea

Mechanical grip = vertical load of the car on the tire (compound + temperature + surface). On flat ground it is fairly steady. Crests, compressions, or camber change it a lot—even with no aero.

Safe method: find the limit by **braking harder at a safe mark**, not by braking later and hoping. Fear blocks learning; early lockup kills session flow.

## Why it works

Braking needs force between rubber and track. Without vertical load there is no clean deceleration. Asking for the same peak at every speed ignores that the mechanical ceiling is not the high-speed downforce ceiling.

Lab on a straight first (later in this module). Then move the reference.

## On track

From 90 to 0 km/h in a low-aero zone, the threshold feels short and steady. Use the same hit you would at 250 km/h and you saturate the front axle too early.

On a crest that ceiling drops: the same 90% can be too much. In a compression you may have margin left.

## Typical mistakes

- Always starting “later” instead of “harder”
- Ignoring elevation and camber
- Blaming setup when the peak is inconsistent
- Expecting the same pedal % at low and high speed
