At high speed the car “weighs” more without gaining inertia. That extra support is **aerodynamic grip**—and it fades exactly while you are braking.

## The idea

Air can create downforce: it pushes the car into the ground and increases force at the tire contact patch. It rises with speed and falls as you decelerate. It does not replace mechanical grip—it amplifies it while you are fast.

That is why on high-downforce open-wheelers and GTs the braking ceiling is a **moving target**.

## Why it works

At the start of a fast stop there is lots of load → a high legal peak. As speed drops, aero stops helping and the same pedal % exceeds the mechanical limit. If you do not release, you lock or trip ABS late—the classic mistake.

## On track

A high-wing formula or GT: at 270 km/h the brake can bite cleanly near 100%. Hold that 100% down to 120 km/h and the front saturates. Peak and terminal pressure (near a stop) can differ a lot.

## Typical mistakes

- Holding the high-speed peak until a slow turn-in
- Releasing too much and sitting under the limit (also loses time)
- Copying a touring-car profile onto an open-wheeler
- Treating release as “what comes after” instead of part of braking
