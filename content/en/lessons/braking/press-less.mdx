The hardest part of braking is not pressing—it is **releasing** with precision. Press Less means keep pushing, just less and less—without “lifting” the foot.

## The idea

The pedal has resistance. Press less and it rises on its own. Use the push-down muscle group, not the lift group. Mentally: 100 → 99 → 98… feeling the same push ease off.

First train a ridiculously slow release. Once you own that, speeding the release up is easy. You decide **what** to do with the brake, not whether you can release it.

## Why it works

Thinking about “taking the foot off” creates a harsh step. On high DF, releasing too much leaves you soft under the limit; releasing too little locks late. Press Less builds muscular feel to follow the moving ceiling.

It also stops constant adjustments that muddy the reference: a shaped release beats a thousand corrections.

## On track

After the peak on a straight, instead of snatching the foot away, you ease the same push millimeter by millimeter. In an open-wheeler this avoids a valley that is too deep after the peak. In a touring car it avoids the street-style “stab and dump”.

## Typical mistakes

- Thinking about lifting the foot
- Releasing in a panic step
- Soft peak then pressing harder near the corner
- Too many adjustments: you never know if the mark is good

<Exercise>
**8** stops: peak at max clean and a conscious release as slow as possible for at least 2 seconds.

Then **8** with a faster but equally smooth release.

Log how many had a step. Goal: **zero** steps in the second block.
</Exercise>
