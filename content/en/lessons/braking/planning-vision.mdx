Without good vision, brake and turn-in are improvised when grip is already committed. This lesson defines **planning vision**: look far enough ahead to decide—but not so far that the information stops being useful.

## The idea

Planning means looking where the car will be in **about one second**. That horizon gives you time to program brake, turn-in, and exit. It is not “stare at infinity”: if you look at the end of a hill you cannot use yet, you miss the detail that matters now.

Information must be **useful**: marks, curbs, bumps, the start of the braking zone. If you only stare at the hood, every decision arrives late.

## Why it works

At high speed, the gap between seeing and acting eats meters. Bad vision means late braking, late turn-in, and abrupt corrections. The brain needs early data to open its effective field: curbs, dirt, elevation, trees or boards that anchor the reference.

Planning moves the decision into a zone with more grip and attention margin. Assessment (next lesson) then checks whether the plan held.

## On track

A 240 km/h straight into a 2nd-gear corner. You do not stare at the end of the next hill. You look at the 100 m board, then the entry curb. That sequence is useful planning: each glance feeds the next input.

In a battle the same rule saves you: inside, offline, you need time for a plan B. Without horizon you only react to the mirror.

## Typical mistakes

- Looking too far (useless scenery)
- Staring only at the hood or locking onto the apex too early
- Forgetting the exit while planning only the entry
- Returning to short-vision autopilot as soon as pace rises
