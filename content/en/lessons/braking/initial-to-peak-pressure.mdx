Braking has phases. If you mix **onset** and **peak** on the same mark, you never know what you are measuring—and consistency dies.

## The idea

1. **Onset:** pedal contact (squeeze, do not kick)  
2. **Peak:** maximum useful pressure  
3. **Release:** toward turn-in (Press Less)

You want the peak quickly, but under control. Between onset and peak the car still covers meters. A slow or inconsistent ramp-up makes your “150 m mark” sometimes onset and sometimes peak.

## Why it works

Light, stiff cars accept a quicker squeeze. Heavy, soft cars a slightly slower one—but measured in “blinks”, not half a straight. A blink is ~150 ms: think one for formula and about two for low DF / soft suspension. Still determined.

Without distinct visual anchors the foot improvises and entry speed changes every lap.

## On track

You decide: board = onset, bridge shadow = peak, end of curb = turn-in. Even when traffic changes, the brake shape holds.

If you use one mark and the ramp-up sometimes lasts 30 m and sometimes 50 m, consistency is broken even if you “brake in the same place”.

## Typical mistakes

- Kicking the pedal
- One mark without knowing if it is onset or peak
- A 50 m “smooth” ramp that is actually slow
- Moving onset and peak together when you adjust

<Exercise>
Pick **three** marks. Ten stops in order: onset at 1, peak at 2, turn-in at 3.

Wrong order does not count. Goal: **8 of 10** correct sequence, with peak inside a tight % band.
</Exercise>
