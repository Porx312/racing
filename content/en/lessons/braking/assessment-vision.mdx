Things do not always match the mental map. **Assessment vision** is the short glance that checks position and angle—then returns you to the plan.

## The idea

Assessing means looking for an instant just ahead of the car: am I on the curb, is the inside tire where I wanted? A **tenth of a second** is enough. Then you plan again.

The pattern is **plan → check → plan → check**, several times per sector. In blind corners you assess more: you can only plan as far as you can see.

## Why it works

A small exit error becomes a big entry error into the next corner. Without a check you keep executing an obsolete plan. With it, you correct position and angle while grip margin remains—open a degree of wheel, keep a bit more brake, instead of adding lock blind.

## On track

In a compound corner you look at the far apex (plan), then briefly at the curb under your inside tire (check): on it or already wide? You adjust and look to the next reference.

At a blind apex the entry curb is your clock: use position and angle relative to the curb to time turn-in. Next lap you assess whether it worked.

## Typical mistakes

- Only planning and never looking where you are
- Parking your eyes on the curb and forgetting the horizon
- Assessing only after the corner (“it already went wrong”)
- Correcting without naming the symptom (push / slide / ok)

<Exercise>
Pick a sector with two or three corners. For **8 laps**, say `check` every time you assess.

Log how many checks per lap. Goal: at least **4** conscious checks per sector without parking your eyes on the curb.
</Exercise>
