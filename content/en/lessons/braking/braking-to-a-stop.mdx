Straight-line braking to a stop (or nearly) is the **threshold lab**: no corner, no trail, only pressure. If you cannot repeat a clean peak here, you will not have it at turn-in.

## The idea

Ceiling first; move the mark later. The usual mistake is braking later then fighting. Better: same safe mark, **harder**, until you feel the longitudinal limit. Fear blocks; early lockup or ABS kills flow.

With the wheel at zero, almost all of the grip circle is available longitudinally. You isolate the variable.

## Why it works

Low DF: peak may look like terminal—you can hold nearly the same force to the end. High DF: peak is high, then you must release even without turning—the ceiling moves with speed.

Repeating the same peak lap after lap builds confidence before you move the reference closer.

## On track

You arrive at 180 km/h on the pit straight, wheel at 0°, and hunt maximum deceleration without lockup. The reference is not time—it is **peak quality**.

When the peak is stable, only then try moving the mark a little later—not the other way around.

## Typical mistakes

- Starting by braking later instead of harder
- Adding steering “to stabilize”
- Releasing early out of fear before the threshold
- Judging the drill by lap time

<Exercise>
**10** full stops or hard slows from the same speed.

Log lockup/ABS (yes/no) and clean peak %. Goal: **8 of 10** with no lockup and peak variation under 10 percent. Only then move the mark a little later.
</Exercise>
