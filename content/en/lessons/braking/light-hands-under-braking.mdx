Peak braking happens in a straight line with the car balanced left-right. **Light hands** let that happen; a death grip sabotages it.

## The idea

Association: **hard brake = light hands**, at the same time, from peak onset until turn-in starts. The wheel self-centers; the front end adapts to small bumps. Iron hands sneak in 1–2° of lock and kill the peak.

When you start turning, add angle progressively—not as a slap.

## Why it works

The grip circle is limited: ~100% brake means almost 0% turn. Squeeze the rim and you add lateral force and leave 50/50 balance. You also filter tire feedback.

If you exit a corner and brake while keeping the same steering angle, you lock or unsettle. First lighten and straighten; then build the peak.

## On track

In a high-speed stop the wheel tugs from uneven asphalt. With light hands you let it speak and correct 1–2°. With iron hands you add 10° and kill the peak.

Over bumps, soft hands let the car “dance” a little and keep lateral balance.

## Typical mistakes

- Death grip at the peak
- Still turning as you start braking
- Using the wheel to balance a poorly seated body
- “Fixing” with more FFB instead of loosening the hands

<Exercise>
**10** straight-line stops with a light grip (subjective ~3/10).

If you squeeze the rim or steer more than a few degrees, that rep fails. Goal: **8 of 10**. Say aloud each peak: `brake — light hands`.
</Exercise>
